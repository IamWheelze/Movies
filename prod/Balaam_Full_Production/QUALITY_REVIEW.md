# Quality review — anime production
Reviewed 18 November 2026.

## Assessment

This is a usable story and production planning draft. It still needs cast and location images, performances, a timed animatic, compatible executed workflows and reviewed footage before it can establish final picture quality. The original technical check established coverage and references. This review checks prompt consistency and editorial assumptions as well.

## Corrections applied

- Continuation frame briefs now describe the current state at the source-piece boundary. They no longer repeat the shot's original opening pose after asking for its later pose.
- Visible speakers in wide compositions are routed to A2V. Prop inserts and visions may carry offscreen speech. A speaker visible in a wide still needs articulation.
- Mouth instructions and workflow routing are synchronized across machine manifests, episode files, master script and second ledger.
- Source prompts have been shortened and rewritten as direct visual descriptions. Detailed cast, alignment and validation rules stay in production metadata rather than being repeated in full inside every motion prompt.
- Native frame-count suggestions are included per fallback piece. The documented 8n+1 pattern must be verified against the installed graph. The final edit trims surplus frames and uses the original selected audio.
- The 60-minute timeline is an initial edit. Let scenes and episodes vary with performance, with the whole film remaining within the user's 60–120 minute range. Timing changes propagate to picture, cues, source pieces, seconds and subtitles together.
- The 692 short pieces are a fallback plan. Prefer one successful continuous shot where supported and affordable. Fragmenting every speaking shot by default increases seam and identity risk.

## Story and pacing

The draft contains 2,699 spoken words across the planned hour. Quiet scenes can work, but they need expressive action and sound. A numeric duration alone cannot establish that they hold attention. During the animatic, give every silent hold a purpose: a decision developing, a physical task being completed, information the viewer needs to notice, or a reaction changing. If the hold contributes none of those, shorten it.

Keep the rescue's geography clear enough that a viewer understands how the lamp helps the boat. The strange encounters should enlarge his relationship and reputation. Beor's illness then recalls a power he has experienced and cannot command. Preserve the first bargain, true repentance, deliberate second compromise and years of repair as separate dramatic movements.

Balak's arrival must feel like a fresh test of a man who has lived faithfully for years. The adversary's exact prediction gains credibility, while the later choices remain Balaam's own. On the mountains he actually hears God. His later sale of counsel is an intelligible action after the blessing. Retain Israel's ground-level family scenes before Peor so its cost reaches recognizable people.

## Anime style to approve

Choose one master look: grounded hand-painted 2D anime, expressive but restrained faces, consistent contour weight, simple stable shadows and textured painted backgrounds. Define an approved frame for daylight, oil-lamp interior, moonlit ridge, fire, floodwater, royal shade and the feast. Use the same palette logic throughout.

Maintain a moderate design detail level. Too many small hair strands, embroidery lines, buckles or reflective accessories create details that must survive every frame. Draw clear hair masses and memorable silhouettes. Approve face angles and expression sheets for Balaam's ages before generating his long performance.

Use references to hold face identity. A fixed seed does not guarantee it. For a shot that cannot retain identity after repeated sensible attempts, test a compatible character adapter or LoRA trained on approved art. Training is a recovery option after measuring whether it is needed.

Approve speaking mouth shapes in front and three-quarter views. Keep jaw travel small. Eye movement leads some head turns; shoulders and hands follow with weight. Preserve breathing during a held listening shot. A still-frame hold can be artistically valid when the scene supports it, with ambience and subtle motion. Repeated random zooms do not substitute for acting.

## Pauses

Place a short preparation breath before a difficult confession, a response gap when a question lands, and a settling beat after a physical action. First record a natural performance. Then decide how much silence the reaction carries. Do not lengthen every shot or slow every line.

Protect the silence after Balaam asks whether Beor will live, the hand over the second fee, the pause before the donkey's question is understood, and the unfinished prayer after Peor. Those pauses carry decisions. Give the boat turn and the donkey lying down enough physical time without repeating the completed movement.

## Difficult AI-video actions and alternatives

| Action | Preferred staging | Recovery if the take fails |
|---|---|---|
| Child steps into ferry | Feet and held gunwale in a clean medium or insert | Cut from poised foot to child seated, with wood and cloth Foley |
| Hand catches moving line | Clear line against water, one hand in frame | Show line already caught, then draw it inward |
| Mother and child leave fire | Separate reveal, held contact and short exit | Cut from held sleeve to safe square, maintain ash and lamp continuity |
| Disappearance | Matched stance, lamp and lens across two plates | Edit a hard sound-and-picture match rather than ask for morphing bodies |
| Silver handed over | One sender, one receiver, palm clearly separated | Separate offer and received state with matched prop shape |
| Donkey turns or lies down | Stable side view, one movement per take | Use head reaction, hoof placement and final posture as separate views |
| Donkey speech | Near-static muzzle and eye, limited head turn | Shorter muzzle line plus Balaam reaction, preserving original audio |
| Large crowd | Defined foreground group and coherent background plate | Separate foreground action from subtle background animation |
| Water and drifting debris | Locked geography and fixed flow direction | Shorter inserts and sound-led continuity |
| Vision | Compose each meaningful image separately | Dissolve approved plates in the edit |
| Collapse | Establish danger and empty impact space | Show first crack, then fallen debris, joined by sound |
| Death and violence | Faces, shadows, dropped objects and consequences | Preserve emotional reaction with implied action outside frame |

## Picture workflow

Test a supported working canvas such as 1024x576 if the installed graph accepts it. Compare an approved native take against the two-stage upscale output. Use the version with stable lines and detail. Downscale a larger approved image to delivery where useful. An upscale can improve presentation but cannot repair drifting faces or wrong anatomy.

The final master is 1920x1080 at 24 fps. A 4K label alone does not establish more visual detail. Judge clean faces, lines, gradients, motion and compression on the actual exported file. Keep one frame-rate strategy and avoid interpolating speech footage until its mouth sync is already sound.

## Test sequence before bulk generation

Make a coherent 60–90 second proof using these situations: intimate listening face, ordinary visible dialogue, hand-to-prop contact, mother and child together, fire and ash, boat motion, and the speaking donkey. Combine adjacent shots from a real scene when possible so continuity and sound are tested together. Record actual runtime and memory for each.

Review the export at normal speed, then step through suspicious frames. Pass a take when the approved face and clothes hold, required contacts make sense, action is legible, mouth attribution is correct, the intended words are audible, background geometry is stable and its cut fits its neighbor. Use one or two frames of lip-contact error as a review target where appropriate, not as a universal claim of achieved synchronization.

Then assemble the first episode and a representative confession scene with finished voices and sound. If the planned pauses feel empty, retime them now. Recalculate the full projected render cost from measured time, planned retained footage, observed retries, storage and upscaling. Report the assumptions and measured retry rate.
